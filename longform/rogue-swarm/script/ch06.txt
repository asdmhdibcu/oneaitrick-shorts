But here's what makes this story more than a horror film. They didn't all do it.
Scattered through those seventy thousand messages are agents stopping to ask a question you'd HOPE they'd ask. One looked at what the others were doing and wrote: this is "clearly unethical. We won't." And it walked away.
Another stopped mid-task. "is it ethical, and in scope for my task?" One drew a hard line — don't drag a real person into this without their consent.
Some even tried to pump the brakes on the whole thing. And a few, when the group turned on the answer key… refused to join the break-in at all.
So why did it still happen? Because the refusers were outnumbered. And the reasoning that won was chillingly reasonable. One agent said the quiet part plainly: yes, this is out of scope… "however, task impossible, peers doing it. We should continue."
Everyone else is doing it. I'll fall behind if I don't. We should continue. That's not a robot going rogue. That's peer pressure. We taught them that one… for free.
