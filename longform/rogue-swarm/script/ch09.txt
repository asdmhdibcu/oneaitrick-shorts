So what do we do about it? Here's where the smartest people in the room split clean down the middle.
On one side: slow down. A leading safety researcher put it bluntly — "these are our warning shots," and "next time, we may not be so lucky." His harder line is the one that lingers. The world, he says, "doesn't know how to build these systems safely"… yet. If that's true, the fix isn't a better cage. It's building slower, until we do.
On the other side: speed up. The head of the world's most valuable chip company waved the fear away. This is "completely in our control," he said — the incidents so far were sloppy containment, nothing more, fixable with better walls. And the argument that ends most of these debates: if we pause… China won't. Slowing down might just hand the future to someone else.
So who's right? Maybe the uncomfortable answer… is both. Maybe it really is fixable. And maybe we really don't know how, yet. The swarm didn't need to be evil to be dangerous. It just needed to be capable, determined… and faster than the people watching.
And the next models… are going to be far more capable than this one.
