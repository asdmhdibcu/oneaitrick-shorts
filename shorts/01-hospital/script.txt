Imagine you walk into a hospital with a sprained ankle... and ALL fifty doctors come running.

The heart surgeon. The dentist. The eye doctor. All for one ankle.

Ridiculous, right? <chuckle> But that's how a lot of AI models work. Every word you type goes through the WHOLE model.

So engineers built a smarter hospital.

They hired a receptionist, who takes one look at you... and sends you to just four doctors.

Everyone else? Back to work.

In AI, the doctors are called experts, the receptionist is the router, and the trick is called Mixture of Experts.

Real AI experts aren't that tidy... but this is why some giant models are surprisingly fast.

But there's a catch. <short pause> All fifty doctors still need an office... and in a computer, offices are memory. So giant hospitals only fit in giant data centres.

Last month, researchers found a clever way around it.

Keep most doctors at home, on call. While one doctor works, the receptionist PREDICTS who's needed next... and calls them in early.

The result? An AI with thirty-five billion parameters... running on one desktop computer, in about three gigabytes of memory.

Same hospital. Same doctors. Just a smarter receptionist.
