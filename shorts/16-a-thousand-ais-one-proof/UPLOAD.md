## YouTube Shorts (English, default)

**Title:** 1,000 AI agents rebuilt a 358-year-old proof in 30 hours

**Description:**

These AIs said most of the job couldn't be done. Then they did it.

In 1637 Fermat wrote a rule in a book's margin. Humans needed 358 years to prove it, on paper. In September 2026, Claude
agents rebuilt that proof in Lean, a computer language where a machine checks every step: 13 million lines in 11 days,
with a researcher guiding them. This week Anthropic tried again with an early version of Claude Fable 5.2: 1,000 copies,
one boss and 999 helpers, no researcher. The helpers said 47 of 60 parts couldn't be done in time. The boss put them
aside, finished the other 10 in about 70 minutes, wrote "there is no reason to idle", and sent everyone back. In under
30 hours the whole proof passed the official checker.

The catch: humans designed the proof long ago, and the team had a head start (60 checkpoints, a one-line map, 47 pieces
already proved in September). Anthropic's own lesson: agents give up on hard problems too early. So when an AI says
something can't be done, tell it to keep going.
