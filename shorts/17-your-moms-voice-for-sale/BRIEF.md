## A new format: the story spine

From #17 the creator asked for engagement through a storyboard the viewer can follow, not faster cuts ("some of the
visuals go through very fast that my eyes don't really see it"). The rules for this short:

- **One stage the viewer relates to:** their own phone, centred (y 380–1180), on screen from the first frame to the
  last.
- **A chapter tracker** at the top (y ~290): CALL · COPY · MARKET · BANK · LOCKS. Each chapter lights up when it
  starts and stays ticked.
- **Pinned facts:** each key number lands as a small card beside the phone and stays there. The last ~8 s recap the
  pins before the end card.
- **"You" from the first line**, and a last frame that mirrors the first: the same "Mom" call, and this time you ask
  for the word.
- **Holds:** every visual on screen at least 2 s; text held at about 3 words per second; one new thing at a time;
  nothing under 1 s.

These become standing repo rules (CLAUDE.md, MOTION.md) once #17's numbers show they work.

## The story

Hook → evidence → twist → takeaway.

- **Hook:** "Nearly ONE in three people say a fake voice called them." It scored 19/20 against "It's Mom. She's
  crying. And it isn't her." (18/20; #12's Trial Reel used the same opening) and "Thirty seconds of your voice is
  enough to copy it." (16/20).
- **Everyday picture:** a key. Mom's voice is a key: you hear it, the door opens. Copying a key only needs a good
  look at it; copying a voice, a short clip. The tools that cut copies are now sold. Your bank code is the second
  lock (the FTC's own doorknob-and-deadbolt picture). The fix is two locks no copy can open.
- **Twists:**
  - 0:23: the app this channel uses says 30 seconds of audio can be enough, and this narration is a clone of the
    creator's voice.
  - 0:29: the key-cutting machines are for sale (trade up over 7× in two years; stolen cloning accounts from about
    $10).
  - 0:40: what these criminals discuss most isn't the family call, it's fake bank phone lines that ask for your code.
- **Catch at 0:52:** the numbers come from companies that sell protection, and they count sales posts and people's
  own reports, not stolen money.
- **Takeaway:** two locks. A family safe word agreed face to face, and a code that never leaves your phone (hang up,
  call a number you know).
- **Hidden word:** **MANGO**, the example safe word on the recap card and on the last frame. The voice says "the
  word", never the word.
- **First frame:** the phone ringing with "Mom" on screen, the claim "1 IN 3 GOT A FAKE-VOICE CALL" above it, chip
  "SURVEY · 12,000 PEOPLE · 6 COUNTRIES · HIYA", chapter 1 lit.

## Left out on purpose

- Named fraud tools and their prices (Saint OTP Bot ~$30 a week, Gorilla's Call Bot $200 per 1,000 calls, Berry AI
  Voice Clone $400–500): naming them advertises them.
- Hiya's US-only "one in four Americans": the six-country figure fits a worldwide audience.
- The $5,400 "kidnapped daughter" case: one US news story, and the story already has a family call.
- "Listen for odd pauses": the FBI lists it, but the short's whole point is the FTC's "Don't trust the voice"; the
  fix is the lock, not the ear.
