## The story

Hook → evidence → twist → takeaway.

Five researchers from Carnegie Mellon, UC Berkeley, Oxford, MIT and UCLA gave 1,222 adults in the US short sets of fraction or reading problems, with an AI helper (GPT-5) in a sidebar for about half of them. After about ten minutes the AI was taken away (in the first experiment, without warning) and everyone did three problems alone. The people who had the AI solved fewer problems in all three experiments, and in two of them they also skipped more: 20% against 11% in fractions, 8% against 1% in reading. People who said they had used the AI for hints did about as well as people who never had it, though that part can't prove cause. Told as one story on one stage, drawn as a 1950s classroom cartoon: a pencil riding a bike along a worksheet line, with the AI as its training wheels.

**Everyday picture:** training wheels. They hold you up so well that you never learn to balance; take them off and you wobble. The AI is the turquoise pair of training wheels on the pencil's bike from the first frame, and hints are a hand on the back of the seat that lets go. The pencil and its bike are the stage itself, on screen from the first frame to the last.

**Evidence:** once the AI was gone, its users solved fewer problems than people who never had it in all three experiments (57% against 74% in fractions, 71% against 77% in the repeat, 76% against 89% in reading). They skipped more in two of the three (20% against 11%; 8% against 1%).

**Twists:** 0:11, the wheels come off without warning. 0:20, it happens in reading too: 8 skips in 100 against 1. 0:30, people who only asked for hints did about as well as people with no AI.

**Catch (0:37):** short tests (sessions of about 13–15 minutes, tested straight after), so nobody knows yet whether it lasts or adds up; the hint result is a clue, not proof, because people chose how to use the AI. The AI here was built to give full answers whenever asked.

**Takeaway:** ask your AI for a hint first, not the answer: a hand on the seat that lets go.

**Hidden word:** GRIT, printed on the pencil's barrel from the first frame to the last (large on the close-ups and in the recap). The voice never says it.
