## The story

- **Hook:** 450 applications. 19 interviews. Zero offers.
- **The real thing (said plainly):** companies aren't firing young people over AI. They are hiring fewer of them, mostly in jobs where AI does the task itself. Applicants and employers both use AI now, so applications pile up and software filters most of them out.
- **Whose story:** Irene Chang and Jacqueline Kline (NPR), Sabrina Highfield and Adam Mitchell (NBC News). Their real words, names and numbers; no photos of them.
- **Evidence:** Stanford's payroll study (ADP), Greenhouse's numbers, Britain's Institute of Student Employers, the Bank of Korea.
- **Twist:** older workers in the same AI-heavy jobs grew about 10%; it's hiring, not firing; Korea shows the same thing.
- **Catch:** not everyone thinks it's AI (remote work; the biggest AI spenders hired more beginners); Stanford says pattern, not proof; education halves the gap; Korea has fewer young people.
- **Takeaway:** get the knowledge you only get by doing (internships, apprenticeships, real projects); one carefully chosen application beats a hundred sent by a bot (Greenhouse: about 5× the hire rate). It's not you.
- **Hidden word:** GHOSTED, in the status column of the opening spreadsheet. Never said.
- **First frame:** the spreadsheet counter landing on 450, with Offers: 0 in red.
